# HW01 – Finding an idea and exploring the problem

**FigJam board:** FIGJAM_LINK (section "HW01 – Problem exploration")

## What was completed

- Problem map on FigJam: the problem, who it affects, the situation, the user goal, the organization goal and the most important constraint.
- One-sentence problem statement.
- Two verifiable success criteria.
- Assumption map. Every key claim is labelled with its source, and the riskiest assumption is marked.
- A short visual pitch.

## The essence

**Problem statement.**
> When a university student has a few spare minutes between classes, on the tram or before sleep (*situation*), they want to spend them on something interesting they will still remember later (*goal*). But the apps within thumb reach are built to keep them scrolling (*obstacle*), so a short break turns into half an hour of content they can't recall, and they come away feeling they wasted the time (*consequence*).

**User goal vs. organization goal.**
- *User:* in a spare moment, find one thing worth reading, read it or keep it for later, and then put the phone down.
- *Organization* (an independent side project): send readers to the original blogs instead of keeping them in the app. Keep running costs low by generating each summary once per article. Earn trust without engagement tricks: "time well spent", not "time in app".

**Most important constraint.** No engagement mechanics. There is no infinite feed, no streaks, no like counts and no notifications, and there is a daily limit. This rules out the usual retention tools. A second constraint is that the articles belong to their authors: the app shows a summary and an excerpt and links to the original.

**Success criteria (verifiable).**
1. In a moderated test, at least 4 of 5 participants start from the phone's home screen and, within 3 minutes and without help, find one article they say is worth their time and either read it or save it.
2. After seeing the end-of-day screen, at least 4 of 5 participants can explain in their own words that today's set is finished and when new articles arrive, and they do not look for a way to get more.

**Riskiest assumption.** *"In the moment a student reaches for a social feed out of habit, they would open a calmer reading app instead."* Every other assumption depends on this one, and so far there is no evidence for it, only my own experience. HW2 interviews target it first.
