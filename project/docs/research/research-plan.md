# Research plan (HW02)

*One page. Sessions run with **AI-simulated participants** (instructor-approved); see [SIMULATED-PARTICIPANTS.md](SIMULATED-PARTICIPANTS.md). The plan below is written as it would be for real students, so the method can be repeated with them.*

**Design decision this research informs.** Should the product be built around *short spare moments* (open the phone, get a few good reads, stop)? Or does reading happen in a different situation, where a save-for-later list or a digest would fit better? The answer decides the main task and the direction chosen in HW04.

**Research questions** (open, not about my idea)
1. What do students actually do on their phone in short breaks, and how do they end up in that app?
2. How do they feel about that time afterwards, and what do they remember of it?
3. When do they read longer articles or blog posts, if ever? Where do they find them, and what makes them start or give up?
4. What have they already tried to control their phone use (limits, deleting apps, other apps)? What worked, and what didn't?

The riskiest assumption from HW01 is #4: "in that moment they'd open a calm reading app instead." It is tested *indirectly*, through questions 1, 3 and 4, about past behavior. I won't ask "would you use…?".

**Method.** Semi-structured interview, 15 minutes, in person or on a video call, using critical-incident questions ("tell me about the last time…"). In the same sitting, a 5–10 minute label comprehension test for HW05. Notes are written by hand during the session and typed up the same day. Nothing is recorded.

**Participants.** 3–5 university students aged about 19–26 who use their phone during breaks in the day. I'll try to include at least one non-IT student, so the sample isn't only people like me. Recruited from classmates and friends of friends, with no incentive. Limitation: a small convenience sample, so the results are indicative, not representative.

**Data handling.** I ask for verbal consent using the script, and nobody is recorded. Notes use codes (P1–P5) instead of names, and I only keep age range, field of study and phone habits. No names, contacts, app account names or screenshots of people's phones. Raw notes stay on my laptop. Only the anonymized notes go into the repo and onto FigJam. Participants can withdraw, and their notes are then deleted.
