# Session A: interview + label check (≈25 min)

*Used with 4 AI-simulated participants (SIM-P1 to SIM-P4); see [SIMULATED-PARTICIPANTS.md](SIMULATED-PARTICIPANTS.md). It's ready to reuse with real students.*

Bring: this script, the note template (`notes-template.md`), and the label cards (section C) on paper or one per screen.

## A. Consent (read aloud, ~1 min)

> Thanks for helping. This is for a university UX course. I'm learning how students use their phone in short breaks. It takes about 25 minutes. There are no right or wrong answers, and I'm not testing you. I'll take written notes but won't record anything, and I won't write down your name. You can skip any question or stop at any time, and I'll delete your notes if you ask. Is that OK with you?

Note "consent: yes" with the time. If they say no, stop.

## B. Interview (~15 min)

Ask about **past, concrete** behavior. Don't mention the app idea until the very end, and don't ask whether they'd like it.

**Warm-up**
1. What do you study, and what does a typical weekday look like? *(Note only the field of study and rough age range.)*

**Short breaks** (research questions 1–2)
2. Think about the last time you had a few free minutes today or yesterday, for example between classes or on the tram. What did you do on your phone? Walk me through it.
3. How did you end up in that app? Did you decide to open it, or did it just happen?
4. How long did it last, compared with what you intended?
5. What do you remember from that time now?
6. How did you feel right after you put the phone down?

**Reading** (research question 3)
7. When did you last read a longer article or blog post, something more than a short post? Where did you find it?
8. What made you start reading it? Did you finish it? If not, what stopped you?
9. Do you ever save things to read later? What happens to them?

**Control** (research question 4)
10. Have you ever tried to change how you use your phone, for example with screen-time limits, deleting apps, or turning off notifications? What happened?
11. Is there anything about those few free minutes you wish were different?

**Follow-ups to use anywhere:** "Can you tell me more?" · "Why do you think that is?" · "Can you show me?" (only if they offer) · Stay silent for 3 seconds before moving on.

## C. Label check (~7 min, for HW05)

Say: "Now something different. I'll show you some words from an app's menus and buttons. For each one, tell me what you'd expect to find or to happen if you tapped it. There are no wrong answers. If a word is unclear, that's the label's fault, not yours."

Show **one label at a time**, without context. Write their answer word for word.

| # | Label shown | What we intend it to mean (don't say this) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Today** | Today's limited set of article cards |
| 2 | **Saved** | Articles kept to read later (also offline) |
| 3 | **Not interested** | Hide this article and show fewer like it |
| 4 | **Daily limit** | How many cards you get per day (setting) |
| 5 | **Topics** | Subject areas you want in your feed |
| 6 | **AI summary** | A short summary written by AI, not by the author |
| 7 | **Open original** | Read the article on the author's own website |
| 8 | **12 of 30** | You've seen 12 of today's 30 cards |
| 9 | **Done for today** | The set is finished; new cards arrive tomorrow |

Then a quick preference check. Show the alternatives together and ask: "Which word would you look for if you wanted to keep an article and read it later?" Choices: *Saved / Read later / Bookmarks / Library*. Ask the same about the main screen: *Today / Feed / Discover / For you*.

## D. Close (~2 min)

12. *(Only now)* "I'm thinking about an app that gives you a small set of good articles for those spare minutes and then stops. What's your first reaction?" Note their reaction, but **treat it as an opinion, not as evidence of use.**
13. "Can I contact you this weekend for 20 minutes to try a clickable prototype?" (Round B: HW07–HW09.)

Thank them.
