<!--@slide Title-->
# A few good reads, then you're done for today

TechTok (working title) · UX/UI Design · Olt Bajrami

---

<!--@slide Why this topic-->
# A 5-minute break turns into 30

I noticed it in myself: I unlock the phone in a short break and lose it to a feed.

---

<!--@slide Problem-->
# The problem is the situation, not a missing app

Students with short breaks reach for an endless feed by habit. Longer reading feels risky: *is it worth it, will it fit?*

**Result:** the break stretches and nothing is remembered.

---

<!--@slide Research note-->
# Evidence: 4 interviews, 7 tests (all AI-simulated)

Instructor-approved simulated participants. Plausible, not observed. Every claim is marked [SIM].

---

<!--@slide Insight 1-->
# People decide from a preview and the time it takes

"Headlines are clickbait half the time. I need to know if it's worth it." (P4)

→ Every card shows the summary, source and reading time.

---

<!--@slide Insight 2-->
# Limits you can dismiss get dismissed

"The limit pops up and I just press 15 more minutes." (P3)

→ The end is part of the content: a finite set and a calm end screen.

---

<!--@slide Persona and journey-->
# Dóra: the thumb decides before they do

Journey: break starts → unlock → scroll → "already?" → nothing remembered.

---

<!--@slide Direction-->
# Chosen: a finite daily set of summary cards

Picked from 10 ideas on impact × feasibility. Rejected: a break timer (just another limit to dismiss).

---

<!--@slide Feedback-->
# "12 of 30" read as a quota, "18 left" as running out of lives

Test results: 0/3 understood progress without pressure.

---

<!--@slide Iteration-->
# "12 of 30 seen today · no need to see them all" passed

Retest: 1/1. "More like a page count in a magazine."

Limit: one simulated participant.

---

<!--@slide Success criteria-->
# Main task: 3/3 unaided in under 3 minutes · end screen: 3/3 understood

Target was 4 of 5. The sample is 3 simulated users.

---

<!--@slide Demo-->
# Demo: one break, one article, done

Live Figma prototype · backup: recorded video

---

<!--@slide Limits and next step-->
# Untested: will they open it instead of TikTok?

Next: a 5-day diary study with 5 real students.

---

<!--@slide Reflection-->
# What I learned

Strongest evidence: the progress-label iteration.
Weakest: everything rests on simulated people.
I'd test with real students before building anything.
